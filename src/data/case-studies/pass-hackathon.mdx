# How a 12-person team runs a 400-hacker event

> **Draft case study.** Based on an organizer interview from February 2026. Attribution is organization-level; details are pending review with the organizing team before final publication.

## Snapshot

PASS Hackathon 2026 at UC Santa Cruz ran **January 14–16** with **over 400 attendees**, **six speakers**, and sponsors including NordVPN, Calshi, N8N, Framer, and OpenNote — organized by just **12 core members** plus 4 volunteers, mentors, and judges. They received **500+ applications**, raised **~$33.5K**, and ran a room that was **56% first-time hackers**, mostly local to Santa Cruz.

## What they do differently

**They use an event to recruit their organizers.** Rather than interviewing for initiative, they pilot a "Hack Day" mini-event in mid-October and have prospective organizers help run it — a working tryout that filters for follow-through before anyone joins the core team.

**They carry a decade of institutional memory.** The team structure is president → directors → organizers, with a **board that includes people who started the hackathon 10 years ago** plus supporting faculty. Continuity isn't left to chance.

**They design for first-timers as the majority.** At 56% first-time hackers, the beginner experience is the main product — light-touch acceptance (any well-written, serious application gets in), heavy promotion through the engineering school, and beyond-the-laptop programming like a weather balloon launch coordinated by their logistics director.

## How they run it

- **Timeline:** organizers finalized by September, Hack Day in October, then a **three-month build** — venue and resources confirmed, sponsorships worked until the final weeks, portals built by the engineering team as a steady workstream. Run of show locked **two weeks out**, in late December.
- **Funding:** cold email via directories, LinkedIn outreach, and personal connections; a dedicated sponsor email plus a spreadsheet tracking every send and response; pitch sequence of interest email → call with stats and deck → sponsorship package.
- **Communication:** **Discord for participants** (easy onboarding), **Slack for internal ops**, weekly Sunday meetings, and direct emails between directors and organizers.
- **Applications:** 500+ responses split evenly across all 12 organizers, everyone reads.
- **Judging:** ~3 judges per team across 100+ teams, Google Forms rubric with aggregated scores.

## What surprised them

Mid-event, a medical emergency (a seizure) halted everything — including in-person judging. Because scoring already lived in Google Forms, they **shifted to fully virtual judging on the spot** and continued with only a 30–45 minute delay. Resilience came from the format choice, not from heroics.

Their biggest stated learning matches every team we've interviewed: **clear communication.** Their version of the story was two directors pursuing NVIDIA concurrently without coordination — the fix being that everyone knows who owns what, and developments are shared across departments immediately.

## The one thing to copy

**The Hack Day organizer tryout.** One small October event solves three problems at once: it tests prospective organizers on real work, gives the team a live rehearsal of their systems, and markets the main event — all three months before game day.
