# Why AI Is Changing the Way We Run Hackathons

The hacker's journey has transformed. The organizer's playbook is next — and it's already happening quietly.

## The transformation you haven't heard about

We have all witnessed the transformation in the hacker's journey — from vim to ChatGPT, from dev tools and platforms to terminal coding agents. A participant at a 2026 hackathon builds in a weekend what would have taken a team a month five years ago.

But there is surprisingly little insight and conversation around the **organizer's perspective** — around how to actually run a hackathon with AI.

It's already happening. **TreeHacks** is using AI to bold and surface key details in applications, so reviewers spend their attention on judgment instead of scanning. **Hack the North** built an AI bot in their Slack channels that resurfaces past questions organizers have already answered — so nobody repeats the same work twice. Organizers everywhere are quietly adopting AI across:

- **Operations** — managing logistics and coordinating teams
- **Sponsorships** — building custom CRMs and outreach tools
- **Marketing and promotion** — creating content at scale
- **Judging** — rubric alignment and score aggregation tools like Harp and Jury

And yet, most content about "AI + hackathons" stays surface level — setting up an AI assistant, or using AI to build the event website. This guide takes a more nuanced approach: **practical, scientifically backed suggestions that are rooted in real organizer experiences.**

## Where this guide comes from

This guide is built on an ongoing research project: interviewing **100 hackathon organizers** and publishing case studies that become a practical playbook for designing, running, and improving hackathons.

The research is led by Chinat Yu, a hackathon researcher at Stanford exploring how we can make learning more fun, personal, and impactful. Over the past 5 years he has attended more than 50 hackathons across the world, published a Stanford white paper on running inclusive hackathons, and written a master's thesis on improving the hacker learning experience.

The work is done in collaboration with **freeCodeCamp** and Quincy Larson, whose 11M+ community will co-publish the case studies and the finished playbook.

## What the fundamentals still are

One theme from the interviews so far deserves to be stated up front. When we asked a logistics lead who has run events from 30 to 3,000 hackers what changes with scale, the answer was: the fundamentals don't.

- **Cultivate community.** The event is a room full of people before it is a schedule of activities.
- **Encourage building and collaboration.** The energy of staying up all night shipping something with friends is the product.
- **Promote trying new technologies.** Hackathons are where people take risks they wouldn't take at work or in class.

AI changes the cost of the operational work around those fundamentals — it doesn't change the fundamentals. The best organizers we've interviewed use automation to buy back time, and then spend that time with their hackers.

## How to read this guide

The chapters follow the organizer's journey in roughly chronological order — purpose, timeline, team, participants, funding, logistics, experience, and follow-through. Each chapter combines:

1. **Patterns from interviews** — what real organizing teams actually do, attributed at the organization level.
2. **Where AI helps** — specific, non-surface-level applications organizers are using today.
3. **Checklists** — the concrete artifacts you should have at each stage.

You can read it front to back before your first event, or jump straight to the chapter matching the fire you're currently fighting.
