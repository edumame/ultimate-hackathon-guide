# Sponsors and Funding

The sponsor pipeline that actually works: cold outreach, the stats call, the package — and the CRM habits that prevent collisions.

## A realistic number

PASS Hackathon 2026 raised approximately **$33.5K** for a 400+ person, three-day event with a 12-person team. That's a useful calibration point: a campus event at meaningful scale, funded largely through cold outreach, without a famous brand behind it. Their sponsor roster mixed national names (NordVPN, Framer) with startups (Calshi, N8N, OpenNote) — sponsor mixes are usually barbell-shaped like this.

## The pipeline

Every funded team we've interviewed runs some version of the same three-stage pipeline:

### 1. Outreach

- **Cold email via directories** — finding contacts through company directories and sponsor lists from other events' sites.
- **LinkedIn messages** — slower, but higher reply quality for developer-relations contacts.
- **Personal connections first** — warm intros through organizers, alumni, and faculty close at far higher rates. Mine your own team's network before you cold-email strangers.

### 2. The stats call

Don't attach the sponsorship package to the first email. The sequence that works: a short interest email → a **call where you pitch with stats** (attendance, school mix, first-timer rate, past sponsor outcomes) and a tight slide deck → *then* the package. The call qualifies them; the package closes them.

### 3. The package

Tiered packages with concrete deliverables: logo placement, workshop or talk slots, API/challenge prizes, recruiting access (resume books, booth time), and judge seats. Price the top tier against what a sponsor pays for a single conference booth — you're offering a full weekend of engaged developer attention.

## Track everything, or pay for it

The infrastructure both teams used: a **dedicated sponsorship email address** plus a **spreadsheet tracking every contact — when each message was sent, and the response state.**

Why this matters is best told by the failure case we heard: two directors at the same event pursued **NVIDIA concurrently without knowing about each other** — overlapping outreach to the same sponsor, confusion about who owned the relationship and how the pitch would even be presented. It risked the deal and burned internal trust. A shared tracker that every outreach touch gets logged in — before the email is sent — is the fix, and it costs nothing.

## Sponsors are a day-of product too

The pipeline doesn't end at the wire transfer. Assign a sponsor-experience owner for event day: booth logistics, workshop slots honored, intros to strong teams, and a debrief contact. Renewals are won during the event, not in next year's cold email.

## Where AI helps

Sponsorship is the function where organizers told us they're building the most custom AI tooling:

- **Custom sponsor CRMs.** Teams are building lightweight CRM tools with AI-assisted enrichment — pulling company funding stage, dev-rel contacts, and past hackathon sponsorships into the tracker automatically.
- **Personalization at scale.** The difference between a 1% and 8% cold-email reply rate is specificity. LLMs make "personalized paragraph referencing the sponsor's actual product and why your hackers care" scalable to a 200-company list.
- **Prospect research.** "Which companies sponsored hackathons at comparable schools in the last two years" is an afternoon of agent-assisted research instead of a week of tab-hopping.
- **Package drafting.** Generate tier variations and one-pagers per sponsor vertical (infra, fintech, consumer) instead of one generic PDF.

The caution: AI raises the volume ceiling, and sponsors are already drowning in AI-written outreach. Use it for research and drafts; keep the relationship human — the stats call can't be automated.

## Checklist

- Budget with a fundraising target and a minimum-viable number
- Dedicated sponsor email + shared tracker, logged before each send
- One named owner per sponsor relationship — collisions are process failures
- Stats deck ready before outreach starts
- Tiered package priced against conference-booth alternatives
- Day-of sponsor experience owner assigned
