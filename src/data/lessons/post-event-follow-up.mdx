# Post-Event Follow-Up and Community Momentum

The event is a spike; the community is the asset. Closing the loop with hackers, sponsors, and your own team.

## The two-week window

Everything in this chapter is worth half as much if it happens a month late. Memory fades, momentum dissipates, and sponsor contacts move on. Budget the post-event sprint into the plan — it's the last two weeks of the [timeline](/organizers-timeline), not an afterthought.

## Close the loop with hackers

- **Results and recordings out fast.** Winners, photos, project gallery, and any ceremony recordings within days.
- **Feedback survey immediately** — response rates collapse within a week of the event. Keep it short, mostly structured, one free-text field that you'll actually synthesize.
- **Celebrate continuation.** The highest-value outcome of a hackathon is a project that keeps going. Feature teams that continue building; connect them to accelerators, clubs, or grants. Ask about continuation explicitly in your survey — it's a metric worth tracking year over year.
- **Keep the community alive.** The Discord doesn't die at closing ceremony. Seed it: share opportunities, other events, and alumni updates. Your next event's marketing starts in this channel.

## Close the loop with sponsors

Sponsors renew based on what they can show *their* boss. Send each sponsor a short report:

- Headline numbers: attendance, first-timer rate, projects submitted, workshop attendance
- Engagement specifics for *their* sponsorship: challenge submissions, booth traffic, API usage
- Photos of their presence, and 2–3 standout projects relevant to them
- A renewal ask while the event is fresh

This is an afternoon of work per event that materially changes next year's fundraising baseline.

## Close the loop with your team

- **Retro within two weeks.** What worked, what broke, what the next team should know. The Berkeley team's multi-semester commitment model and the Santa Cruz board of founding members are both, at bottom, continuity mechanisms — the retro doc is the cheapest one.
- **Archive the artifacts.** Trackers, run of show, vendor contacts, budget actuals, emergency plan — one folder, findable by people who weren't there.
- **Thank-yous that mean something.** Volunteers, mentors, judges, venue staff, faculty champions — personal notes, not a mass email. These people are next year's roster.

## The advice worth ending on

When we asked an organizer of one of the world's largest collegiate hackathons what they'd tell someone running their first event, the answer had nothing to do with logistics: the fundamentals are the same at 30 people or 3,000 — cultivate community, encourage building and collaboration, get people trying new technologies. And when it gets stressful mid-event, slow down and remember it's for the hackers, the people, and the community.

The energy of staying up all night and seeing friends from around the world build things — that's the constant. Everything in this guide exists to protect it.

## Where AI helps

- **Survey synthesis.** Hundreds of free-text responses clustered into themes with representative quotes, in minutes. This turns the feedback form from a ritual into an instrument.
- **Sponsor report generation.** The report structure above is templatable — feed the event stats and per-sponsor engagement data, get a draft per sponsor, edit for warmth, send.
- **The retro-to-memory pipeline.** Retro notes, decision logs, and archived docs become the corpus for the institutional-memory bot ([see Communication Systems](/communication-systems)) that onboards next year's team.
- **Content at scale.** Recap posts, a project-gallery writeup, thank-you drafts, and next-event teaser content are a single working session with an LLM instead of a week of procrastination.

## Checklist

- Results, photos, and gallery published within days
- Feedback survey sent within 48 hours, synthesis shared with the team
- Per-sponsor report with renewal ask, within two weeks
- Team retro held and archived within two weeks
- Artifact folder complete for next year's team
- Community channel seeded with a reason to stay
