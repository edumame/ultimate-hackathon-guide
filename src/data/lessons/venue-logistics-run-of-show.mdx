# Venue, Logistics, and Run of Show

The three-month build, the two-week run-of-show lock, and planning for the emergencies you can't predict.

## Venue first, everything after

The venue is the longest-lead decision and the one that constrains all the others: capacity caps your admissions, overnight policy shapes your schedule, catering rules shape your food plan, and A/V determines your ceremonies. Lock it before the three-month build begins ([see the timeline](/organizers-timeline)).

Questions to resolve in writing with the venue, not on event day:

- Overnight access and sleeping areas — is this actually a 24/36-hour event?
- Power density and wifi capacity at full load (hackers bring 2–3 devices each)
- Food service rules, loading docks, and trash logistics
- Security requirements and emergency procedures
- Which rooms you *actually* control, hour by hour

## The build checklist

From the interviews, the three-month workstream breaks into parallel tracks:

- **Venue and resources** — confirm rooms, furniture, power, signage
- **Food** — vendors, dietary coverage, delivery timing. Field note: the teams we interviewed deliberately over-ordered snacks. A surplus is cheap insurance; a shortage is what hackers remember.
- **Portals** — registration, check-in, and submission systems, built as a steady workstream by the engineering team, not a final-week sprint
- **People ops** — volunteer recruitment, mentor and judge scheduling, walkie-talkie or radio plan for day-of coordination (the Berkeley team runs their events on walkie-talkies)

Track it all with the weekly all-hands plus direct owner follow-ups — the cadence both interviewed teams independently converged on.

## The run of show

Finalize the run of show **two weeks before the event** — the timing both teams use. Earlier and it churns; later and there's no time to rehearse, staff it, and brief everyone.

A real run of show is minute-by-minute for transitions (doors, ceremonies, judging, meals) and block-based in between, with a named owner per line item. Do a full schedule run-through with the team in the final week.

## Plan for the emergency you can't predict

The strongest logistics story from our interviews: mid-event, a medical emergency (a seizure) forced the Santa Cruz team to halt in-person judging entirely. Because they could fall back to **fully virtual judging via Google Forms**, the event continued with only a 30–45 minute delay.

The lesson generalizes: you can't predict the specific emergency, but you can pre-decide the fallbacks —

- **Judging fallback:** a virtual/asynchronous path that works if the physical process halts
- **Medical:** who calls, who meets responders, who clears the room
- **Schedule slip:** which blocks compress when (not if) you run late
- **People buffer:** volunteers with no fixed assignment who absorb surprises

Write the emergency plan at T-2 weeks. It's an hour of work that buys calm when it counts.

## Where AI helps

- **Run-of-show stress testing.** Feed the schedule to an LLM and ask it to find conflicts: staffing gaps, impossible transitions, rooms double-booked, meal timing colliding with judging. It's a tireless line-by-line reviewer.
- **Vendor comms.** Quote requests, follow-ups, and delivery confirmations across a dozen vendors is exactly the templated-but-personalized writing LLMs do well.
- **Day-of question deflection.** A bot over your logistics FAQ (wifi password, parking, schedule) in the participant Discord answers the 200 questions your organizers otherwise answer individually — the Hack the North internal pattern, pointed outward.
- **Capacity math.** Meals per dietary restriction, tables per room, volunteers per shift — constraint arithmetic that's easy to get wrong at 2 AM the week before.

## Checklist

- Venue contract signed with overnight, power, wifi, and food terms explicit
- Food plan with dietary coverage and deliberate snack surplus
- Portals live and tested before applications close
- Run of show locked at T-2 weeks, owner per line, full run-through done
- Emergency plan written: judging fallback, medical, schedule-slip rules
- Day-of comms hardware (radios/walkies) distributed and tested
