# The Organizer's Timeline

A T-minus countdown from six months out to the closing ceremony, built from how real teams actually schedule the work.

## The three-month core build

Both teams we've interviewed in depth converge on the same shape: a leadership team locked in early, then an intense **three-month build** covering venue, sponsors, and software, with the run of show finalized **two weeks before the event**.

Here's the countdown, cross-referenced with the [MLH organizer guide's timeline](https://guide.mlh.io/general-information/hackathon-timeline), which follows the same arc over a longer runway.

## T-minus 6 to 4 months — Foundations

- Lock the **date and purpose** (see [Purpose and Success Metrics](/purpose-and-success-metrics)). Check the academic calendar, religious holidays, and competing hackathons.
- **Finalize the leadership team.** The teams we interviewed had organizers confirmed by September for winter events. Recruiting organizers late is the root cause of most downstream slips.
- **Lock the venue.** It's the longest-lead item and everything else (capacity, catering, A/V, overnight policy) depends on it.
- Put up a **placeholder website** with the date and an email signup.

## T-minus 4 to 3 months — Money

- Build the **budget** and the **sponsorship prospectus**.
- **Start sponsor outreach now.** Sponsorship runs until the final weeks, but the pipeline needs months of lead time. Set up your tracking system on day one (see [Sponsors and Funding](/sponsors-and-funding)).
- Open **organizer applications** if you're growing the team — one team we interviewed uses a mini "Hack Day" event in October as a working tryout for new organizers.

## T-minus 3 to 1 months — The build

This is the intense stretch. The teams we interviewed set everything up in these three months:

- **Confirm venue details and resources** — rooms, power, wifi capacity, food vendors.
- **Open hacker applications.** Run promotion continuously, not as a one-shot announcement (see [Recruiting Participants](/recruiting-participants)).
- **Build the portals** — registration, check-in, submissions. Engineering teams we interviewed treated this as a steady three-month workstream, not a final sprint.
- **Book judges, mentors, and speakers**; plan workshops with sponsor partners.
- Continue sponsor outreach and start **closing** packages.
- Track everything with **weekly full-team meetings** (both interviewed teams meet Sundays) plus direct 1:1 follow-ups between directors.

## T-minus 2 weeks — Lock it

- **Finalize the run of show.** Both interviewed teams lock it two weeks out — late enough to absorb changes, early enough to rehearse.
- Final headcount → food orders, badge printing, swag counts.
- **Reminder blasts** to participants: logistics, what to bring, waivers.
- Judge and mentor briefings; confirm rubrics (see [Workshops, Mentors, and Judges](/workshops-mentors-judges)).
- **Write the emergency plan** — medical, security, weather. One team we interviewed had a seizure incident mid-judging; their pre-agreed fallback (fully virtual judging) saved the event with a 30–45 minute delay.

## Game day

- Check-in process staffed and tested before doors open.
- Meal distribution with buffer stock — surpluses are cheap insurance, shortages are memorable.
- Judging execution per plan, with the fallback ready.
- Protect organizer "dilly-dally time" — someone should always be free to just talk to hackers.

## After the event

The most neglected phase — it gets its own chapter: [Post-Event Follow-Up](/post-event-follow-up). The short version: thank-yous, sponsor reports, feedback synthesis, and a team retro within two weeks, while memory is fresh.

## Where AI helps

- **Project management copilots.** Feed your timeline into a task tracker and let an agent flag slipping dependencies ("venue contract unsigned at T-3 months blocks the headcount decision").
- **Reminder drafting.** Every T-minus milestone has an email or announcement attached. Draft the entire sequence in one sitting with an LLM, then schedule it.
- **Institutional memory.** Hack the North's Slack bot pattern — an AI that answers "have we dealt with this before?" from past channels and docs — is most valuable during the three-month build, when every week produces a decision someone will re-ask about next year.

## Checklist

- Date, venue, and leadership team locked ≥4 months out
- Sponsorship prospectus and outreach tracker live ≥3 months out
- Weekly full-team meeting cadence running through the build
- Run of show locked at T-2 weeks
- Emergency plan written and shared before doors open
