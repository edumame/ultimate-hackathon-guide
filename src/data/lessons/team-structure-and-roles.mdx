# Team Structure and Roles

How real organizing teams are structured, from 12-person crews running 400-hacker events to 26-person democratic orgs.

## Two real structures

**The 12-person crew.** PASS Hackathon 2026 at UC Santa Cruz ran a 400+ attendee event with 12 core organizers, supported by 4 volunteers plus mentors and judges. Structure: a president, directors over functions (sponsorship, logistics, engineering, marketing), organizers under them, and a board of founding members and supporting faculty providing continuity — the event is 10 years old, and the board carries its institutional memory.

**The 26-person democratic org.** Hackathon at Berkeley runs two events a year (a 3,000+ hacker flagship and a summer AI hackathon) with a 26-person team structured in three tiers: **Cubs** (new members), **Directors** (larger-scale organizers), and an **Executive** tier that sets organizational goals — with 8 sub-teams working across functions. Decisions are made democratically.

The lesson isn't "copy either chart." It's that team size should follow event scale and function count, not ambition. A 400-person event genuinely runs on 12 committed people.

## Recruit for initiative, not headcount

The strongest pattern across interviews: **no arbitrary team-size targets.** The Berkeley team recruits against specific skill gaps, and screens for one trait above all — people with a builder mindset who tackle problems without being assigned them.

Tactics that showed up in the field:

- **Recruit from underrepresented tech groups and past volunteers** — both widen the pool and strengthen community ties.
- **Distribute recruiting forms through the engineering school** — official channels reach students clubs can't.
- **Use a working tryout.** The Santa Cruz team pilots a "Hack Day" mini-event in October where prospective organizers help run something real before joining. It filters for exactly the trait you can't interview for: follow-through.
- **Ask for a multi-semester commitment.** Berkeley requires at least three semesters — organizing knowledge compounds, and continuity is how it compounds.

## Culture beats incentive structures

Neither team we interviewed has a formal incentive or accountability system. The phrase that stuck with us, from the Berkeley team: they're "a bunch of friends that just so happen to organize a hackathon together." They rely on self-motivated people who hold themselves to high standards — which is precisely why the recruiting filter matters so much. You can't add accountability later to people who needed it.

What replaces process:

- **Weekly full-team meetings** (Sundays, in both teams) as the sync point.
- **Direct personal follow-ups** between directors and organizers — not everything routes through the meeting.
- **Clear ownership per function** — the failures we heard about were ownership collisions, not laziness (see [Communication Systems](/communication-systems)).

## Roles you actually need

For a first event, cover these functions (people can hold two):

1. **Lead / president** — tiebreaker, external face, owns the purpose
2. **Sponsorship** — pipeline, pitches, sponsor day-of experience
3. **Logistics** — venue, food, supplies, run of show
4. **Engineering** — website, registration, check-in, submissions
5. **Marketing** — promotion, socials, comms to participants
6. **Experience** — workshops, mentors, judges, mini-events

## Where AI helps

- **Onboarding new organizers.** The three-semester rule exists because context takes time to transfer. An AI assistant over your past docs, decision logs, and Slack history (the Hack the North pattern) compresses that ramp dramatically.
- **Meeting leverage.** Auto-transcribed weekly meetings with action-item extraction mean the Sunday sync produces an artifact, not just alignment.
- **Coverage planning.** Generating and stress-testing the day-of staffing grid ("who covers check-in during the opening ceremony?") is exactly the kind of constraint problem LLMs handle well.

## Checklist

- Functions mapped to named owners before the three-month build starts
- Recruiting pipeline that tests initiative (tryout event, trial task)
- Multi-semester or multi-event commitment expectation set upfront
- Weekly all-hands cadence with owned action items
- A continuity mechanism: board, alumni advisors, or a decisions doc
